Ancient societies classification

A Sociologists and anthropologists classify societal groups based on their access to resources, prestige, or power. At the bottom end of the societal scale are clans, followed by tribes, chiefdoms — and at the highest are the states.

B Clans consist of a small group of people who are typically hunters or gatherers. Members are typically related to each other through marriage or descent and the group as a whole does not have any formal ranks of leadership. Today there are few societal groups that fit the profile, and most are relegated to areas like the Hadza of Tanzania. They hunt animals with bows and arrows and create their own shelter with dried grass and branches.

C Tribes are mostly larger than clans. They tend to nourish mostly on cultivated plants and domesticated animals. Individual communities integrated through kinship ties.

D Chiefs are graded on a scale of prestige. Prestige and rank are determined by how closely a person is related to a senior chief.

E Statesmen share many of the characteristics of a chief but with one key difference: they tend to have absolute power as a king or a queen. Lower-ranked people have an obligation to pay taxes.

F Elman Service research led him to conclude that class war was not present in ancient civilisation.
